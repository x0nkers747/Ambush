# ADR 0022: Fire-team formation planning

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-09
- Builds on ADR 0012 (one destination and one facing per Marine), ADR 0018 (the engagement
  wedge) and ADR 0020 (viewsheds). Changes neither the simulation nor combat.

## Context

A 13-Marine squad is planned one Marine at a time: select the Marine, click a destination, drag
the facing. Placing a fire team took four of these. The trainee needs to place a whole fire team in
one action, then fine-tune individual Marines.

What the planning system already had:

- **Plans.** One destination and one facing per Marine (ADR 0012). Clicking again moves the
  destination; there is no waypoint chain to append to.
- **Paths.** The path is generated by navigation and is never part of the plan.
- **No undo, no saving in the app.** Plans have a versioned exchange format
  (`serializeDeploymentPlans`) but nothing in the app saves them yet.
- **Roster.** The roster is a flat tree with no expand or collapse.

## Decision

### Fire teams come from the organization

A fire team is a template unit whose echelon is `fireTeam` (`fireTeamsOf`). Its Marines are the
holders of its slots, in the template's slot order. Nothing is hard-coded:

- not soldier ids;
- not the team size;
- not the squad leader, whose slot belongs to the squad, so is in no fire team.

### Selection

The session holds either one Marine (`selectedSoldierId`) or one fire team
(`selectedFireTeamId`), never both.

- **Selecting a team.** Clicking a fire team's name in the roster selects the team. Its name and
  its Marines' rows are highlighted, and so are its Marines on the map, with labels.
- **Switching back.** Clicking a Marine, in the roster or on the map, switches back to that one
  Marine.
- **Clearing.** Esc clears the selection.
- **Selection is not an order.** Selecting never changes a plan.

### The formation is planning metadata over ordinary plans

`Exercise.placeFormation(team, centre, facing?)` gives every Marine of the team an ordinary
destination and an explicit facing.

- **Shape.** The Marines stand in a line through the centre, perpendicular to the facing, with
  `formationSpacingM` between neighbours (default 10 m, an exercise option).
- **Slots.** Slot `i` of `N` is `(i − (N − 1) / 2) × spacing` metres to the right of the centre,
  seen facing the way the formation faces. Slot 0, the fire-team leader, is on the left; then
  the automatic rifleman, the assistant and the rifleman.
- **Geometry.** All of this is in map metres with the one azimuth convention (ADR 0002), so it
  does not depend on zoom.
- **Default facing.** Without a facing given, the formation keeps its previous facing. A new
  formation faces the way the team will walk, from its centre now to the new one.

The Marines' plans remain the only orders. Execution, paths, saving and replay see nothing but
ordinary plans. The exercise keeps, per team, only:

- the centre;
- the facing;
- the spacing;
- the slots;
- which Marines still follow the formation.

None of this is in the simulation, and it is not saved.

### Editing

On the map, with a fire team selected:

| Action                             | Effect                       |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Click                              | Place the team (a new order) |
| Drag the diamond at the centre     | Move the formation           |
| Drag the yellow handle             | Turn it round its centre     |
| Right-click the diamond, or Delete | Delete it                    |

Moving or turning recomputes every following Marine's destination and facing from the centre,
facing, slot and spacing. Deleting clears their destinations and facings.

Because each Marine has a single destination, placing the team again replaces its formation; it
does not append a second one. This keeps the existing replace semantics of destinations.

### Individual Marines

A Marine is _linked_ while that Marine's draft is exactly the plan the formation generated for him. Any
individual edit detaches that Marine for good, and the map says so:

- a new destination or facing;
- a cleared destination or default facing;
- a reset of that Marine's plan.

Group edits then move only the linked Marines and never snap a detached one back. A new click
with the team selected is a new order for the whole team and links everyone again. Once nobody
is linked, the formation is gone.

### Atomic operations

Every slot the operation would change is checked as an individual destination is:

- on the map;
- not in or against a building;
- reachable from where the Marine stands.

If any slot fails, nothing changes, and the refusal names each failing slot. The formation is
never snapped or squeezed to fit, so a drag simply stops at the last position that fits.
Placing, moving, turning and deleting are each one operation on the drafts, so they cannot be
left half done.

### Visibility: the union of exact viewsheds

With a fire team selected, the visibility overlay shows what at least one of its Marines can
see: the union of their viewsheds. Every cell is decided exactly as for a single Marine
(ADR 0020), from each Marine's planned position, on the same 4 m grid (8 m while dragging).

**The problem with four separate viewsheds.** Computing four separate viewsheds one after
another was measured in the browser on the recorded Quantico battlefield, for a team placed on
open ground 150 m north of the ORP. Each Marine's viewshed took about 25 s (75 s of CPU across
three workers), so the team's took about 100 s.

- **Why it is slow.** Each visible cell needs a full trace. Proofs only settle hidden cells.
- **Why it is wasteful.** Marines 10 m apart see almost the same ground: about 38,400 visible
  sample cells each, 39,300 in the union.

**The union is computed as one job.** `ViewshedService.requestUnion` runs it through the same
bands, workers, two passes, cancellation and cache as a single viewshed.

- **Chained Marines.** Each band is computed for one Marine after another. Each skips the cells
  an earlier Marine already sees (`computeViewshedRows`'s `alreadyVisible`, wrapped by
  `computeUnionRows`), and every other cell is decided exactly as before.
- **Exact.** A skipped cell is visible in the union by definition. Proofs only ever show a cell
  hidden, so a skipped cell's missing obstruction hint can cost time but never change a result.
  The tests compare the union with the cell-by-cell union of separate viewsheds.
- **Cached viewsheds reused.** Marines whose own viewshed is already cached (for example,
  selected one at a time before) are not computed again: their viewsheds seed the mask, and if
  every Marine's is cached the union needs no job at all.
- **Cached union.** The union is cached by the Marines' exact positions, in slot order, so
  returning to a formation shows it at once.
- **Never outdated.** Moving, turning or a Marine's own edit changes a position, and so the key.
  The service supersedes the job in progress and drops its replies, so an outdated union is
  never shown as current.
- **Meanwhile.** Until the new union's half-way preview arrives, the team's previous union is
  shown, marked pending. A single Marine's result, or another team's, is never shown instead.
- **One overlay.** The union is drawn as one overlay, so cells seen by several Marines are not
  darker.

**Speed.** Single-threaded on sampled rows of the same four positions, separate viewsheds took
22.1 s and the chain took 6.4 s, with an identical result. In the browser, see Verification.

### Wedges

Each selected Marine's own wedge is drawn (ADR 0018): from that Marine's planned position and
facing, out to that Marine's primary weapon's range, 35° wide.

- **Overlaps.** Each distance band of all the wedges is filled as one path, so overlapping
  wedges do not darken.
- **Edges and labels.** Each wedge keeps its own edges and range arc. A repeated weapon label at
  the same spot is drawn once.

## Verification

`formation.test.ts` covers:

- the geometry: counts 1–5, the four cardinal and diagonal facings, rotation about the centre,
  and spacing;
- membership;
- placement, moving, turning and deleting;
- atomic refusals;
- detaching;
- determinism;
- save and load;
- execution through Combat v1.

`group-viewshed.test.ts` covers the union semantics, even shading, the union job, its preview,
reuse of cached viewsheds, caching, invalidation, superseded work and failure.
`viewshed.test.ts` checks `computeUnionRows` and `alreadyVisible` against separate viewsheds. `formation-planning.test.ts` covers selection, the wedges, and the
pointer gestures at several zoom levels.

## Consequences

- **Fewer actions.** A fire team is placed with one click and turned with one drag, and each
  Marine can still be fine-tuned.
- **One destination per Marine.** There is still one destination per Marine; formations do not
  add waypoint chains.
- **Formations are not saved.** Formation metadata lives in the exercise only. Loading saved
  plans into a fresh exercise gives the same plans, without formations.
- **No roster disclosure.** The roster has no expand or collapse, so there is no disclosure
  control to keep apart from team selection.
