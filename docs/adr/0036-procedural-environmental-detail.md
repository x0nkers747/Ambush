# ADR 0036: Procedural environmental detail

- Status: Accepted; overhead wires (section 7) removed by ADR 0037, which keeps the poles and pylons
- Date: 2026-10-11
- Implements visual milestone B of `docs/3d-visual-realism-research.md`. Builds on ADR 0030
  (geographic features), ADR 0031 (procedural vegetation and the generation worker), ADR 0032
  (the authoritative graded terrain) and ADR 0035 (procedural buildings and their entrances).

## Context

After milestone A the buildings looked like buildings, but the space between them was empty:
roads ran between bare lawns, with no paths to front doors, no street lamps, no poles or wires, no
fences or hedges, and trees only inside mapped woods and tree rows.

The mapped data helps little. The targeted query below returned the following for the recorded
5 km battlefields:

| Battlefield | Street lamps | Single trees | Power towers | Poles | Fences | Other                  |
| ----------- | ------------ | ------------ | ------------ | ----- | ------ | ---------------------- |
| Quantico    | 9            | 48           | 73           | —     | 6      | 21 power lines         |
| Grafenwöhr  | 5            | —            | 27           | 89    | 44     | some benches and signs |

Most detail must therefore be inferred. Inference has to stay plausible:

- rural roads must not become suburban streets;
- woods must not be doubled;
- nothing may be invented that a trainee could mistake for information, such as road names or
  speed limits.

All of it is visual only. Movement, line of sight, ballistics, cover, concealment, scoring and the
After Action Review must not change, and neither may the authoritative terrain.

## Decision

### 1. Two kinds of data

**Authoritative** data is what the simulation uses: roads, building footprints, the graded DEM,
water and the vegetation areas. It is unchanged. Mapped footways, paths, steps and driveways
(`service=driveway`) were already imported as roads of class `path` or `paved`. They are drawn as
roads, as before, and take precedence over anything inferred.

**Decorative** data is new and display only:

- **Mapped details:** the terrain's optional `decor` (`DecorFeatures` in
  `src/sim/environment.ts`). It holds points and lines, each with a stable id (`osm-n…`, `osm-w…`).
  - Points: street lamps, single trees, power poles and towers, traffic signs, benches, litter
    bins, bollards and post boxes.
  - Lines: power lines and minor lines, fences, walls and guard rails.
  - The simulation never reads it.
  - `validateTerrain` only checks that its point and line lists are lists.
- **Inferred details:** generated by the 3D view from the authoritative features, the mapped
  details and the buildings' entrances (ADR 0035). They are never stored, so a changed generator
  never invalidates a saved battlefield, scenario or recording.

Nothing decorative enters a simulation structure: no obstacle, no navigation grid, no LOS volume,
no grading. `src/exercise/display-attributes.test.ts` gives the demo battlefield mapped lamps,
trees and fences, deliberately placed across buildings and roads. With and without them, the
battlefield must grade to identical elevations, give identical LOS roofs and 500 identical random
sight lines, and run to identical events and state hash.

### 2. Import (`src/geo/decor.ts`; importer 1.4.0)

The Overpass query gains targeted statements: only the tags that are drawn, never every node in
the box.

```
node["highway"~"^(street_lamp|stop|give_way)$"]
node["natural"="tree"]
node["power"~"^(pole|tower)$"]
way["power"~"^(line|minor_line)$"]
way["barrier"~"^(fence|wall|retaining_wall|guard_rail|city_wall)$"]
node["traffic_sign"]
node["amenity"~"^(bench|waste_basket|post_box)$"]
node["barrier"="bollard"]
```

**What is kept:**

- trees: `leaf_type` and `height`;
- fences: `fence_type` or `material`;
- walls: `wall` or `material`;
- tagged heights, within plausible bounds;
- signs: the `traffic_sign` value, or `stop` and `give_way` from `highway`.

Lines are clipped to the battlefield. Malformed values are dropped, and points without a position
are counted and reported.

`natural=tree_row` and `barrier=hedge` stay vegetation areas (osm.ts). They take part in line of
sight, so they are not imported a second time as decoration.

**Measured** (full 5 km battlefields, 100 m margin):

| Battlefield | Elements | Download | Query time                | Share of the full OSM download |
| ----------- | -------- | -------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Quantico    | 184      | 63 KB    | 1.4 s                     | +1.4 %                         |
| Grafenwöhr  | 232      | 86 KB    | 15.5 s on a slower mirror | +2.3 %                         |

Parsing is negligible next to the rest of the import.

**Compatibility.**

- Older battlefields have no `decor`. They load unchanged, and every detail they show is inferred.
- Re-importing a battlefield adds the mapped details.
- The package records the detail counts in `processing.decor`.

### 3. The placement system (`decor-context.ts`, `decor-space.ts`)

**Context** (`decorContext`) reads the battlefield and never changes it:

- **Built-up density:** buildings counted on a 50 m grid and read over 150 m, giving four classes:
  - industrial: industrial or commercial land use with at least 2 buildings;
  - town: at least 9 buildings;
  - suburb: at least 4 buildings;
  - rural: fewer than 4.
- Land use, region (ADR 0035), vegetation areas, wood edges and water.
- Slope.
- **Vehicle road network:** roads that are not paths or footways.
  - It finds the nearest road through a segment hash.
  - It finds junctions where three or more road ends meet, where two different roads meet at an
    angle, or where a road ends on another road's side.
- Mapped access ways.
- Building walls and footprints.
- **Heights:** `heightAt` on the authoritative ground. There is no second elevation model.

**Room** (`DecorSpace`) is an incremental 8 m spatial hash of what has been placed: discs for
posts, trunks, doorways and props, and corridors for paths, driveways and barriers. Each query
looks only at nearby cells, so nothing is compared with everything.

**Priorities.** Generators run in a fixed order, and each tests its spots against what
higher-priority generators placed:

1. roads, buildings and water;
2. mapped lamps, power, barriers, signs, furniture and trees;
3. doorways, then inferred paths and driveways;
4. inferred utility poles, then street lamps;
5. inferred front-garden fences and hedges;
6. roadside trees, garden trees and shrubs;
7. inferred small props (mailboxes come with the paths, give-way signs come last).

A roadside tree therefore moves away from, or gives way to, a driveway, a lamp or a doorway, never
the reverse.

**Determinism.**

- Features are processed in id order.
- Every choice comes from hashes of ids and positions, and spatially correlated choices (tree
  patches, species) come from value noise.
- There is no `Math.random` and no clock.
- `DECOR_VERSION` (1) versions the generator independently of the terrain, grading and simulation.
- Tests check identical output with roads and buildings in reverse order.

### 4. Paths and driveways (`decor-paths.ts`)

For each building's entrances (ADR 0035: the door on the wall facing the nearest road, and the
garage door), garage first:

- **Mapped first.** If a mapped path, footway or service road lies within about 2.5 m of a point
  2 m out from the door, nothing is inferred for that entrance.
- **Kind by context:**

  | Context                                | What is inferred                                                                 | Width | Maximum length |
  | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----- | -------------- |
  | Houses and barracks                    | Walkway: concrete (North America) or pavers (central Europe)                     | 1.2 m | 45 m           |
  | Garages                                | Driveway: asphalt or concrete (North America), pavers or gravel (central Europe) | 3 m   | 70 m           |
  | Commercial and institutional buildings | Wide walkway                                                                     | 2 m   | 60 m           |
  | Industrial roller doors                | Paved access                                                                     | 5 m   | 90 m           |
  | Farms                                  | Gravel or dirt track                                                             | 3.5 m | 160 m          |

  Small sheds get nothing. A door already within 0.8 m of the road's edge needs nothing either.

- **Routing (simplest first):**
  - straight out of the door until the road;
  - straight to the nearest road point;
  - out 3 m or 6 m, then to the road, with the corner rounded by a quadratic Bézier (at most 1.5 m,
    or the path's width);
  - a walkway may instead end on the building's own driveway when that is shorter.
- **Refusal.** A route is refused if:
  - it enters a building, or passes within 0.6 m of a wall other than leaving its own door, so it
    neither runs through buildings nor squeezes between them;
  - it crosses water or another road's surface;
  - it climbs more than 25 % (walkways) or 17 % (vehicle routes) over any 3.5 m run, or more than
    70 % in a single 1 m step;
  - it mostly overlaps a path already placed.

  If every candidate is refused, the entrance gets no path. Grades are judged over a few metres,
  so a step at a graded pad's edge (ADR 0032) is allowed and a steep bank is not.

- **Drawing.** Paths are draped on the authoritative ground like the roads (`drapeNetwork`), 2.2–
  3.4 cm above it, under the lowest road layer. They end on the road's centreline, so the road
  covers the join. Corners are rounded and widths constant. The terrain is never changed.
- **Mailboxes.** North American houses get a mailbox where their path or driveway meets the road.

**Recorded battlefields:**

| Battlefield | Inferred paths | Left to mapped access | Refused | Main refusal reasons                           |
| ----------- | -------------- | --------------------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Quantico    | 1,038          | 49                    | 413     | 331 steep banks, 56 squeezed between buildings |
| Grafenwöhr  | 2,400          | 15                    | 653     | 346 buildings, 203 steep banks                 |

### 5. Roadside and garden trees

Trees are vegetation instances: the same models, LOD, tile culling, shadows and material as the
woods (ADR 0031). They are added through the new `extra` option of `placeVegetation`, so roadside
trees look exactly like woodland trees.

**Mapped trees** are planted where mapped:

- conifers for `leaf_type=needleleaved`;
- the tagged height when given;
- not where they lie inside a vegetation area, which already draws them.

**Inferred roadside trees** are sampled every 8 m on each side of every vehicle road except service
roads and tracks. They never appear within 25 m of a wood: the wood dominates there. The chance of
a tree depends on context:

| Context    | North America | Central Europe |
| ---------- | ------------- | -------------- |
| Industrial | 4 %           | 4 %            |
| Town       | 22 %          | 12 %           |
| Suburb     | 30 %          | 20 %           |

- **Rural roads:** patchy clusters, from value noise at 160 m.
- **Major roads:** 60 % of the rate, set 6 m further back.
- **Avenues:** some rural roads (18 % in central Europe, 5 % in North America) are lined for
  stretches. The spacing is 13 m with jitter, and a single species (lime or oak). Rows are irregular
  in spacing, offset and size.

**Species** follow region and spatially correlated patches:

- central Europe: lime, oak and beech, with a few spruce in the country;
- North America: oak, lime (standing in for maple) and beech, with a few pine;
- young trees mixed in.

**Placement rules.**

- Offsets: road edge + 2.4–5.4 m (+6 m on major roads).
- Sizes: 55–90 % of the model's height, crowns ±12 %.
- Exclusions: the vegetation exclusions (carriageway, verge, building clearance by crown, water).
- No trees within 11 m of junctions, nor on slopes over 60 %.
- Clear of posts, paths and driveways, barriers, doorways and each other.

**Around buildings:**

- houses get 1–4 shrubs beside the door;
- houses also get a garden tree behind them (55 % in central Europe, 50 % in North America), and a
  third of those gardens get two;
- institutions get a tree in front, and farms one beside the building;
- industrial buildings get none.

### 6. Street lamps

- **Mapped lamps** are drawn where mapped. They face the nearest road and suppress inferred lamps
  within 30 m.
- **Inferred lamps** stand only along streets: paved or major roads that are not service roads,
  tracks or paths. They appear only where the place is built up (town, suburb or industrial),
  never on rural roads, tracks or forest roads.
  - In suburbs some are skipped: 25 % in North America, 10 % in central Europe.
  - Spacing: 31 m in central Europe, 44 m in North America, 42 m on major roads.
  - They stand on one side of each road (by its id), 0.7 m beyond the verge, with the arm over the
    road.
  - They keep 10 m from junctions and clear of buildings, mapped access ways, paths, doorways,
    poles and props. If the first spot is taken, they shift 4 m along the road.
- **Models:**

  | Model                                        | Where                     |
  | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
  | Residential cobra-head on a 7.5 m pole       | North America             |
  | Dark 4.5 m lantern post                      | Central European villages |
  | Modern 9 m galvanised pole with a curved arm | Major roads               |
  | 10 m industrial floodlight                   | Industrial areas          |

- The lamp glass is a pale vertex colour. There are no lights: no point light is created.

### 7. Utility poles and wires

> Superseded in part by ADR 0037: wires are no longer generated or drawn. Poles and pylons, mapped
> and inferred, stay as described here, as standalone objects.

**Mapped power lines** are drawn as follows:

- Each vertex gets a support: the mapped tower or pole there, else a pylon (`power=line`) or a
  distribution pole (`minor_line`).
- Supports turn along the line.
- Consecutive supports are joined by conductors at their crossarms: four on pylons, three on poles.
- Mapped supports on no mapped line are drawn alone, turned along the nearest road.

**Inferred poles** run along some served roads: streets over 90 m long with buildings along them,
and no mapped minor line within 40 m. The share of roads with poles depends on region:

| Region         | Roads with poles | Model                           | Spacing |
| -------------- | ---------------- | ------------------------------- | ------- |
| North America  | 70 %             | Timber, crossarm and insulators | 42 m    |
| Central Europe | 22 %             | Concrete                        | 48 m    |
| Other          | 35 %             | Timber                          | 42 m    |

- Poles stand on the opposite side to the lamps, 2.2 m beyond the verge.
- They are not placed within 8 m of junctions, in or near woods, or on doorways, paths or other
  posts.
- A refused spot breaks the run.

**Wires** join consecutive supports with a parabolic sag of 1.8 % of the span. A span is left out
(never forced) if:

- it is longer than 80 m (600 m for pylons);
- its ground track passes over or within 1 m of a building;
- any point comes closer to the ground than 4.5 m (inferred), 4 m (mapped minor lines) or 7 m
  (pylons).

All wires form one `LineSegments` draw call: a thin dark line, slightly transparent.

### 8. Fences, walls and hedges

**Mapped barriers** become instanced segments along their lines:

- **Length:** at most 2.4 m each, shorter on slopes so the ground under one segment changes by at
  most 0.35 m.
- **Placement:** each segment is centred on its leg and turned along it, so corners meet at the
  vertices.
- **Model by material:**

  | Material         | Model         |
  | ---------------- | ------------- |
  | Chain or wire    | Chain-link    |
  | Wood or picket   | Board fence   |
  | Metal or railing | Bar fence     |
  | Stone or brick   | Stone wall    |
  | Concrete         | Concrete wall |
  | Guard rails      | W-beam        |

  Where untagged: central Europe gets bar fences and stone walls, North America chain-link and
  concrete.

- **Height:** taken from the tag.
- **Openings:** no segment stands in a building, on a road or access way, on an inferred path, or in
  water.

**Inferred barriers** are front-garden hedges and fences only:

- **Which gardens:** 45 % of houses in central European built-up streets (hedge, bar fence or
  board fence) and 12 % in North America (low picket fence or hedge). The house must be set at
  least 3 m behind the road edge, and only one barrier is drawn per frontage.
- **Where:** along the road edge, across the house's frontage.
- **Gaps:** at paths and driveways, junctions, other barriers, posts and doorways.
- Property boundaries are never invented elsewhere, and buildings are never enclosed.

### 9. Signs and street furniture

- **Mapped signs** are moved from the road's centreline (where they are often mapped) to its edge:
  - a stop sign for `stop`, `DE:206` or `US:R1-1`;
  - a give-way sign for `give_way`, `DE:205` or `US:R1-2`;
  - a plain blue panel for anything else, with no text.
- **Mapped furniture:** benches, bins, bollards and post boxes. Post boxes are blue in North America
  and yellow in central Europe.
- **The only inferred sign:** a give-way sign on a minor street where it ends on a major road, on
  the right of arriving traffic, facing it. The context strongly supports it.
- No speed limits, names or directions are ever invented.

### 10. Rendering

Lamps, poles, pylons, barrier segments, signs and furniture are procedural props (`prop-models.ts`:
prisms and boxes in muted vertex colours, three LODs each). They are added to the vegetation
library as a new kind, `prop`, so they share:

- its instancing: one shared material, one `InstancedMesh` per model and band, drawn only when
  something is selected;
- its 100 m tile frustum culling and its distance bands.

Each prop has a `lodScale` (0.15 for furniture up to 1.2 for pylons), so small things stop being
drawn sooner. Thinned props keep their size, unlike plants, whose crowns widen. Paths add one mesh
per surface style, and wires one line mesh. Nothing is regenerated per frame and no light is
added. Disposal frees the path and wire geometry and materials with the rest of the layer.

Everything is generated in the generation worker with the other features:

- `buildFeatures` returns the geometry plus the detail instances;
- `generateBattlefield` hands those instances to `placeVegetation`.

`GEOMETRY_VERSION` is now 3, `PLACEMENT_VERSION` 2 and `DECOR_VERSION` 1.

## Consequences and limitations

- **Inferred, not surveyed.** Lamps, poles, trees, garden fences and paths are plausible for their
  place, not records of what is there. Where mapping exists it wins, but in the recorded data it is
  sparse.
- **Paths are straight or one-bend routes.** They do not curve round gardens or follow terrain
  contours. An entrance with no acceptable route gets no path. Routes that cross a mapped sidewalk
  are allowed, but do not join it.
- **Simplified wires.** Inferred lines follow one side of a road and break at junctions, gaps and
  refused spans. No service drops to houses are drawn. Wires are one-pixel lines, which alias at a
  distance.
- **Mapped barriers are drawn as segments.** Long perimeter fences produce many instances (about
  5,000 at Grafenwöhr). They are instanced and culled, but they dominate the prop count.
- **Hedges along roads** are inferred only in front gardens. Mapped hedges remain vegetation areas,
  planted with shrubs as before.
- **No night lighting.** Lamps have no emissive light and cast nothing. A night mode would need a
  separate, budgeted approach.
- **No speed limits, names or directions are invented.** Signs are mapped ones, plus give-way signs
  inferred where minor streets join main roads.
- **Software rendering only.** No real GPU was available, so frame-rate costs were not measured,
  and the software renderer's timings are not representative of one.
